# F-031 — Scientific Theory of Hierarchical Metric Periodicity

Status: CANONICAL

---

## Purpose

This Foundation defines metric periodicity from the scientific perspective of
Jazz Groove Analyzer. It distinguishes physical temporal evidence,
observation-derived periodicity, metric-level interpretation, metric
reconstruction, and independent scientific validation.

It defines scientific concepts only. It introduces no algorithm, software
component, architectural boundary, validation metric, or Ground Truth
quantity.

---

## 1. Scientific Motivation

Musical performance unfolds through temporally distributed events produced by
one or more sources. These events need not recur at a single interval.
Different recurrent temporal relationships may coexist, overlap, alternate,
disappear, or become locally prominent.

A population of event times may therefore exhibit several forms of recurrence
at once. Short intervals may describe closely spaced events, longer intervals
may describe recurrent groupings, and still longer intervals may describe
relationships among those groupings. None of these temporal scales acquires
musical metric meaning solely by existing or recurring.

A single summary period cannot, in general, preserve this structure. It may
describe one statistical property while discarding:

- the coexistence of distinct interval populations;
- their relative phases;
- their persistence through time;
- their relationships across sources;
- their grouping relationships; and
- their possible nesting.

The scientific problem of metric reconstruction is consequently not reducible
to finding one representative interval. It requires a strict distinction
between:

1. temporal evidence present in the performance;
2. periodic relationships supported by that evidence;
3. interpretations assigning those relationships a metric role; and
4. a reconstructed metric representation built from such interpretations.

This distinction prevents a measured or calculated temporal quantity from
being presented as a musical beat, meter, tempo, or metric level without
sufficient evidence.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Observation

An observation is a scientifically traceable representation of a physical
property obtained from the recorded performance without assigning it musical
metric meaning.

An observation may contain measured time, magnitude, confidence, source
identity, or other preserved physical evidence. Its scientific identity
depends on its provenance and the procedure by which it was obtained.

An observation is not a beat, meter, tactus, subdivision, or metric level
merely because it occurs in time.

### 2.2 Temporal Event

A temporal event is an observation associated with a determinate temporal
position or interval in physical time.

A temporal event records occurrence. It does not, by itself, state why the
occurrence happened or what metric function it possesses.

Several temporal events may occur simultaneously or nearly simultaneously.
Such events remain scientifically distinct when their observational identities
or source provenance differ.

### 2.3 Temporal Periodicity

A temporal periodicity is a recurrent temporal relationship supported by a
population of temporal events.

A periodicity is not a physical event. It is an evidence-derived relation among
events. Its scientific description must identify at least:

- the observations from which it is derived;
- the temporal scale at which recurrence is found;
- its temporal scope;
- the evidence supporting recurrence; and
- its uncertainty, variability, or limitations where known.

Temporal periodicity does not inherently imply musical meter.

### 2.4 Candidate Period

A candidate period is a temporal duration for which observed evidence supports
possible recurrence but for which no musical metric role has yet been
established.

A candidate period is a falsifiable scientific proposition about temporal
organization. It must remain traceable to the observations supporting it.

Several candidate periods may coexist. Their coexistence is not, by itself,
evidence that they form a musical hierarchy.

### 2.5 Metric Level

A metric level is an interpreted position occupied by a recurrent temporal
relation within a nested organization of temporal scales.

A metric level is not directly observable. It is assigned only when evidence
supports a relationship between a candidate periodicity and a broader metric
organization.

A metric level need not initially receive a notational label such as whole
note, quarter note, or eighth note. Such labels require additional musical
evidence linking the reconstructed hierarchy to a conventional metric
representation.

Metric-level interpretation is musical interpretation and therefore belongs
to the Domain under AD-008. This Foundation prescribes no internal Domain
component or implementation form.

### 2.6 Metric Interpretation

Metric interpretation is the assignment of metric meaning to one or more
evidence-supported temporal relationships.

Metric interpretation may establish relationships such as relative level,
grouping, nesting, phase, continuity, or correspondence with a declared
musical reference. Every interpretation must identify its observational basis
and remain distinguishable from that basis. It must not alter the underlying
observations.

### 2.7 Metric Reconstruction

Metric reconstruction is the formation of an inferred temporal representation
from preserved observations and explicit metric interpretations.

A reconstruction is not a corrected performance and is not a replacement for
the observations. It is a scientific model whose claims must remain traceable
to evidence and testable against independent reference material.

Reconstruction may represent continuity where observations are sparse, but
such continuity remains inferred rather than observed.

### 2.8 Hierarchical Metric Periodicity

Hierarchical metric periodicity is the organization of two or more
evidence-supported temporal periodicities into distinct, related metric
levels.

The hierarchy exists scientifically only when the proposed relationships among
its levels are supported by evidence. Numerical multiple or submultiple
relationships alone are insufficient to establish musical hierarchy.

A hierarchy may be local, may change through time, and may remain partially
indeterminate.

### 2.9 Ground Truth

Ground Truth is an independent, authoritative scientific reference used after
blind analysis to evaluate a completed analysis representation.

Ground Truth does not generate observation-derived periodicities, choose
candidate periods, or determine reconstruction during blind analysis. It
supplies independent evidence against which an already completed
interpretation may be tested.

### 2.10 Evidence-Supported Observed Periodicity

An evidence-supported observed periodicity is a recurrence relation derived
exclusively from observed temporal evidence, before musical metric meaning is
assigned.

The word `observed` qualifies the origin of its evidence. It does not mean that
the periodicity itself is a directly measured physical event.

An evidence-supported observed periodicity may be global or local, stable or
variable, source-specific or collective. Those properties must be established
from evidence rather than presumed.

### 2.11 Interpreted Periodicity

An interpreted periodicity is an observation-derived candidate periodicity to
which a metric role has been assigned through explicit, evidence-supported
interpretation.

Its representation must preserve the distinction between:

- the temporal relationship supported by observation;
- the metric meaning assigned to that relationship;
- the evidence and reasoning supporting the assignment; and
- unresolved alternatives and limitations.

## 3. Fundamental Principles

### Principle 1 — Observation precedes interpretation

Metric interpretation may operate only on preserved observational evidence.
An interpretation cannot become the source of the observations used to
justify it.

### Principle 2 — Events and relations are epistemically distinct

Temporal events are observed occurrences. Observation-derived periodicities
are relations derived from populations of events. A relation must not be
reported as though it were an independently observed event.

### Principle 3 — Periodicities must be evidence-supported

A candidate periodicity must originate from reproducible temporal evidence. It
must not be introduced solely because a conventional musical model expects it.

### Principle 4 — Multiple periodicities may coexist

The presence of one recurrent temporal scale does not exclude other recurrent
scales. No single scale is privileged without additional evidence.

### Principle 5 — Metric level is interpretation

A temporal duration, recurrence rate, or numerical ratio does not
independently establish beat, subdivision, tactus, measure, or notational beat
unit.

### Principle 6 — Hierarchy requires relational evidence

A set of candidate periods becomes a metric hierarchy only when evidence
supports their organization into related levels. Numerical proximity to simple
ratios may motivate a hypothesis but cannot alone establish the interpretation.

### Principle 7 — Reconstruction preserves its observational basis

Metric reconstruction must retain traceability to the observations and
observation-derived periodicities upon which it depends. Reconstruction must
not erase contradictory or unresolved evidence.

### Principle 8 — Physical and interpreted time remain distinguishable

Observed timestamps remain properties of the recorded performance. Positions
within an inferred metric organization are interpretations of those
observations. One must not silently replace the other.

### Principle 9 — Indeterminacy is a valid scientific result

When available evidence supports more than one metric interpretation, or
supports none uniquely, the scientific result is indeterminate. Missing
meaning must not be supplied by convention.

### Principle 10 — Interpretation remains falsifiable

Every proposed metric level and hierarchy must expose what evidence would
contradict it. A claim insulated from independent validation is not a
scientific metric interpretation.

### Principle 11 — Ground Truth remains independent

Independent musical reference material may test an interpretation only after
blind reconstruction. It must not determine the candidate periodicities or
hierarchy being evaluated.

### Principle 12 — Reproducibility includes provenance

A periodicity or interpretation is scientifically reproducible only when its
source observations, temporal origin, source identity, analytical conditions,
and transformations remain traceable.

## 4. Scientific Consequences

### 4.1 Average event spacing is not musical beat

For chronologically ordered event times \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\), consecutive
intervals are:

\[
\Delta_i=t_{i+1}-t_i
\]

Their arithmetic mean is:

\[
\bar{\Delta}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\Delta_i
\]

This quantity describes the mean spacing between consecutive events in that
population. It does not establish recurrence, stable phase, grouping, metric
level, beat unit, or correspondence with a notated beat.

When several interval populations coexist, their mean may lie between
recurrent durations and may itself possess little recurrent support. The mean
also loses the ordering, source attribution, phase, multimodality, and temporal
locality of the intervals from which it was calculated.

Therefore:

\[
\text{average event spacing} \neq \text{musical beat}
\]

This inequality expresses a difference in scientific meaning, not merely a
possible numerical discrepancy.

### 4.2 Recurrence is necessary but not sufficient for metric meaning

A recurrent interval may provide evidence for a candidate period. It does not
alone identify the candidate as tactus, beat, subdivision, or measure.

### 4.3 Numerical ratios are hypotheses, not identities

If two candidate periods approximate a simple numerical ratio, that
relationship may support investigation of hierarchical organization. It does
not, without further evidence, establish that the periods occupy particular
musical levels.

### 4.4 A regular reconstruction does not prove a regular performance

A reconstructed regular temporal reference is an inferred model. Its
regularity cannot be attributed to the performance unless preserved
observational evidence independently supports that regularity.

### 4.5 Validation must evaluate temporal and semantic claims separately

A reconstructed period may correspond numerically with an authoritative
reference while remaining semantically unidentified. Conversely, a correctly
identified level may exhibit local temporal variation.

Scientific validation must preserve the distinction between temporal,
hierarchical, and metric-level correspondence and unresolved semantic identity.

## 5. Hierarchical Periodicity Model

The model defines an epistemic progression:

```text
Observed temporal events
          ↓
Observation-derived candidate periodicities
          ↓
Evidence-supported relationships among candidates
          ↓
Metric-level interpretation
          ↓
Metric reconstruction
          ↓
Independent scientific validation
```

This is not a software pipeline and does not prescribe architectural
components.

Observed temporal events provide time-located evidence whose physical
positions and provenance remain preserved. Candidate periodicities describe
recurring relationships without assigning metric identity. Relationships among
candidates may include relative duration, phase alignment, nesting, recurrence,
or coordinated occurrence across sources; they provide evidence for possible
hierarchy but do not name metric levels.

Metric-level interpretation identifies a candidate's role within a proposed
organization and must state alternatives when evidence does not select
uniquely. The resulting reconstruction preserves its evidence lineage. Only
after completion may independent validation support, reject, or leave
indeterminate the candidate, its relationships, its assigned level, or the
resulting reconstruction.

## 6. Relationship with Existing JGA Authority

### Observation Layer and Observable Metric Context

The Observation Layer remains responsible for physical temporal evidence and
introduces no musical interpretation under AD-006.

Observable Metric Context preserves temporal evidence, recurrence, alignment,
and organization. It does not identify meter, tempo, ensemble Pulse, or metric
level. Those terms must not be inferred from observational recurrence alone.

### Translation Layer

The Translation Layer preserves observational information while crossing into
Domain representations. It neither selects a metric interpretation nor
supplies missing hierarchy.

### Domain and metric reconstruction

The Domain owns musical interpretation under AD-008. This Foundation does not
prescribe any internal service, entity, contract, or implementation.

The current authoritative lineage remains:

```text
ElementaryMetricEvent
→ BeatReference
→ MetricCluster
→ Pulse
→ InternalMetricTimeline
```

BeatReference remains a canonical inferred scientific construct reconstructed
from collective evidence and never directly observed or externally imposed.
This Foundation neither redefines nor duplicates that lineage.

### Behaviour representation

Behaviour remains a description of temporal and collective properties derived
from the reconstructed scientific representation. Behaviour evidence may test
stability or change within a proposed reconstruction, but it does not
retroactively transform interpretation into observation.

### Scientific validation boundaries

The Immutable Analysis Representation remains the frozen validation-facing
account of completed analysis. Ground Truth remains independent. The Comparator
compares only approved quantities. The Scientific Validation Record preserves
completed comparison evidence. This Foundation changes none of those
responsibilities or contracts.

## 7. Falsifiable Predictions

1. If a reconstructed metric periodicity arises from the recorded performance,
   reproducible evidence supporting its temporal scale must be recoverable from
   preserved observations without Ground Truth.
2. If candidate periodicities form one metric hierarchy, controlled
   observations must provide reproducible relational evidence beyond isolated
   numerical similarity.
3. Where several recurrent interval populations coexist, general event spacing
   need not identify any one metric level. Consistent unique identification by
   event spacing alone across controlled datasets would weaken the necessity of
   hierarchical discrimination.
4. A periodicity attributed to ensemble organization must remain traceable to
   reproducible relationships among source observations.
5. A proposed hierarchy must produce testable expectations under controlled
   removal or alteration of observations while the experimental reference
   remains controlled.
6. An assigned metric level must be testable against independent authoritative
   reference material after blind reconstruction.
7. When observations cannot discriminate among competing metric levels, a
   scientifically valid result must preserve that indeterminacy.

Failure of the specified evidence or independent correspondence falsifies the
associated claim within the scope of the examined performance. It does not
alter the underlying observations.

---

## 8. Scope

This Foundation does not claim that every performance contains multiple
dominant periodicities, that every recurrent relationship is metrically
meaningful, or that simple numerical ratios establish hierarchy.

It does not identify musical beat, redefine BeatReference, infer beat unit from
period alone, require globally constant organization, prescribe conventional
notation, or permit Ground Truth during blind reconstruction.

It does not define a period estimator, candidate selector, statistical method,
source-consensus rule, hierarchy selector, metric-level classifier, tolerance,
score, software component, API, contract, or pipeline.

Its sole claim is that JGA metric periodicity is an evidence-supported temporal
relation whose musical level, hierarchical role, and reconstruction status are
explicit, independently testable interpretations.
