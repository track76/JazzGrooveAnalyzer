# F-032 — Scientific Theory of Candidate Periods

Status: CANONICAL

---

## 1. Purpose and Experimental Basis

This Foundation answers one scientific question:

> What is a Candidate Period from the scientific perspective of Jazz Groove
> Analyzer?

A Candidate Period is an observation-derived temporal relation supported by
reproducible recurrence evidence within a declared scope, before any musical
metric role is assigned.

This Foundation extends F-031. It defines no algorithm, representation,
software component, architecture or musical interpretation.

Its experimental basis is Scientific Validation Campaign 1 experiment
`H-VAL001-C1-03`, preserved at:

- `validation/VAL-001/run_20260809_100843/`

That experiment established, within its declared scope, that:

- multiple observation-derived periodicities coexist in VAL-001;
- candidate populations are numerically reproducible;
- candidates can be preserved before metric interpretation;
- Ground Truth can evaluate a frozen candidate population after blind
  discovery; and
- arithmetic mean event spacing is not itself necessarily a recurrent
  candidate.

These findings motivate Candidate Period Theory. They do not assign musical
meaning to any discovered period and do not establish that every performance
contains multiple candidates.

---

## 2. Epistemic Position

The scientific progression is:

```text
Observed temporal events
        ↓
Recurrence evidence
        ↓
Candidate Period population
        ↓
Future metric interpretation
```

Observed temporal events are physical observations. Recurrence is a relation
derived from those observations. A Candidate Period is therefore not a
directly observed physical event; it is a falsifiable temporal proposition
supported exclusively by observational evidence.

Metric interpretation remains scientifically later and epistemically
distinct.

---

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Recurrence

Recurrence is the repeated occurrence of a temporal relation within a declared
observation population and scope.

Repeated intervals constitute evidence because more than one occurrence
supports the proposition that a temporal relation recurs rather than appearing
as one isolated duration. The scientific description of recurrence must remain
traceable to its observations, temporal units, measurement conditions, scope
and limitations.

Recurrence does not establish musical significance. This Foundation defines no
equality rule, tolerance, threshold, clustering procedure or statistical
method.

### 3.2 Candidate Period

A Candidate Period is a temporal duration supported by reproducible recurrence
evidence within a declared observation population and scope, before any
musical metric role is assigned.

Its scientific identity includes both the proposed duration and the evidence
supporting it. A duration without recurrence evidence is not a Candidate Period
under this Foundation.

A Candidate Period must remain traceable to:

- its supporting observations;
- its temporal units and measurement conditions;
- its observation, source and temporal scope;
- its provenance and discovery protocol;
- its reproducibility evidence; and
- its limitations.

A Candidate Period is not automatically beat, tempo, tactus, subdivision,
meter or metric level.

### 3.3 Candidate Population

A Candidate Population is the complete collection of Candidate Periods
supported by one declared observation population under one declared discovery
protocol.

The population preserves multiplicity. It must not be reduced to one preferred
period merely because one candidate has a greater occurrence count, lies near
an arithmetic mean, appears in several sources, approximates a simple ratio or
corresponds numerically with Ground Truth.

Candidate populations from different inputs, scopes, measurement conditions or
protocols are not automatically equivalent.

---

## 4. Fundamental Principles

### Principle 1 — Observation-derived relation

A Candidate Period originates from observed temporal events but is not itself
a directly observed physical event.

### Principle 2 — Recurrence before candidature

A temporal duration becomes a Candidate Period only when reproducible
recurrence evidence supports it under a declared protocol.

### Principle 3 — Preserve multiplicity

When observations support several Candidate Periods, the complete supported
Candidate Population must be preserved. No candidate is privileged without
scientifically independent justification.

### Principle 4 — Reproducibility, provenance and scope

Candidate evidence must be reproducibly recoverable and traceable to its
observations, inputs, conditions, protocol and declared scope. Unexplained
failure to recover that evidence weakens or falsifies the candidate claim.

### Principle 5 — No musical metric meaning

Recurrence, frequency, numerical ratios, source support and Ground Truth
correspondence do not independently make a Candidate Period beat, tempo,
tactus, subdivision, meter or metric level.

---

## 5. Blind Discovery and Post-Blind Evaluation

Blind Candidate Discovery establishes and freezes a Candidate Population
without Ground Truth or musical reference information participating in
discovery.

Ground Truth must not supply durations, define recurrence, select candidates,
rank candidates, assign labels or remove candidates during blind discovery.

Post-Blind Evaluation examines the frozen Candidate Population against
independent Ground Truth or other approved reference evidence. It may preserve
numerical correspondence, disagreement or indeterminacy. It must not
retroactively alter the discovered population.

Ground Truth validates Candidate Period evidence. Ground Truth does not
generate Candidate Periods.

## 7. Falsifiability

This theory makes four testable predictions.

### Prediction 1 — Recurrence recoverability

The recurrence supporting a valid Candidate Period must be recoverable from
its preserved observations. Failure to recover it falsifies the candidate
under the declared protocol.

### Prediction 2 — Reproducibility

Repeated blind execution on the same authoritative observations under the same
declared conditions must reproduce the Candidate Population according to the
protocol's declared equivalence criterion. Unexplained failure qualifies or
falsifies the reproducibility claim.

### Prediction 3 — Multiplicity

Where observations support several recurrent temporal populations, blind
discovery must preserve more than one candidate. If the supposed recurrence
cannot be reproduced, the corresponding multiplicity claim is falsified.

### Prediction 4 — Blind independence

Ground Truth availability after discovery must not alter the frozen Candidate
Population. If Ground Truth changes blind discovery, blind independence is
falsified.

---

## 8. Scope

This Foundation does not define:

- beat, tempo, tactus, subdivision or meter estimation;
- metric hierarchy or metric-level interpretation;
- candidate selection or ranking;
- recurrence tolerances or confidence thresholds;
- statistical estimators or discovery algorithms;
- Domain objects, architecture, services, APIs or schemas; or
- implementation milestones.

It does not claim that every recurrent duration is musically significant,
that every performance contains multiple Candidate Periods, that the most
frequent candidate is preferred, that numerical ratios establish hierarchy or
that Ground Truth correspondence establishes metric identity.
